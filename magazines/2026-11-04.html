Critique Labels 'Astra Improving Qwen' Post Misleading

Source: original article

The 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is misleading · honnibal.dev Stage magicians often do the same trick multiple ways. One iteration uses a gimmick prop, the next one shows you the prop isn’t gimmick but that one palms the object, the third time was a forced choice etc. When you watch the performance you assume there’s only one technique, so each time they do it they seem to debunk your hypothesis about how it must be working. This is pretty much how the experiments in the ‘Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen’ post work.

Actionable Insight

The 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is criticized for using experimental techniques akin to stage magic, creating an illusion of a single, novel method. This approach is deemed misleading, as it appears to debunk hypotheses about the system's workings while obscuring the actual underlying mechanisms. The critique highlights a potential lack of transparency in the original post's experimental design and presentation.

Community Voice

Hacker News commenters largely agree the original 'Astra' post is misleading and oversold, particularly regarding claims of improving Qwen's math abilities, noting it primarily reduces reasoning loops. Some argue that the original post did explain its methods for those who read thoroughly, while others view the 'Astra editing Qwen' techniques as a regurgitation of existing concepts. Despite the skepticism, some users remain impressed by the 'Weight-Space Programming' idea, finding it eye-opening and seeing potential for future advancements through methods like RL'ing models on such tasks.

Source: original article

Fujifilm to boost tape production 5x as AI fuels archiving demand - Ars Technica Size Small Standard Large Width * Standard Wide Links Standard Orange In an announcement released Thursday, Fujifilm said it will invest roughly 10 billion yen (about $63 million) to increase its production capacity fivefold for an unlikely beneficiary of the AI era: magnetic tape. Actionable Insight

Fujifilm's substantial investment in magnetic tape production underscores the immense data storage demands generated by AI technologies. This unexpected resurgence of an older storage medium highlights how AI's 'exponential growth' in data is reshaping infrastructure needs across the industry. It suggests that cost-effective, high-capacity archival solutions like tape are becoming critical for managing the vast datasets AI models produce and consume.

Community Voice

Hacker News commenters discussed the practicalities of tape archiving, emphasizing the importance of verifying data integrity by reading back archives. There was also interest in more affordable pro-sumer tape drive solutions and skepticism regarding the sole attribution of demand to AI, with one user suggesting BluRay M-Disks as a potentially cheaper and more resilient alternative.
