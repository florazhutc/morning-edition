Actionable Insight

The proposition that all AI-generated code should be open source sparks significant debate, with existing copyright law often cited against automatic open-sourcing. Source: original article

Lovable: Many Apps Expose Sensitive Data — Crissy Field GmbH Lovable: Many Apps Built on the Platform Expose Sensitive Data AI makes it easier to build your own web applications, but handling sensitive data still requires special care. We examined 159,041 Lovable projects and found many cases where personal and confidential information was accessible without logging in. Lovable is a Swedish AI startup founded in Stockholm in 2023 by Anton Osika and Fabian Hedin.

Actionable Insight

Apps built on the Lovable platform were found to expose sensitive user data, including passport details and private messages, due to insufficient security configurations. This incident underscores the critical need for robust data handling practices and secure defaults, especially as AI simplifies application development for users who may lack deep security expertise.

Community Voice

The community discussion centered on the root cause being insufficient Row-Level Security (RLS) in apps, with some commenters highlighting potential culpability from Supabase's default configurations. Skepticism was voiced regarding the security model of frontend-to-backend communication with RLS, alongside a general concern about the average developer's understanding of data security. There was a strong call for platforms to implement secure-by-default configurations, least-privilege APIs, and automated security testing to prevent easily exploitable vulnerabilities in AI-generated applications. A minor debate also arose concerning whether the incident, where data was publicly accessible via a shipped read-only API key, constitutes a 'breach'.

💻 Hardware Issues

MacBook Pro OLED Displays Reportedly Suffer From Low PWM Flicker

Source: original article

The MacBook Pro’s OLED PWM Problem: When a ‘Better’ Display Becomes Worse to Use Apple has brought tandem OLED technology to the MacBook Pro , joining the Mini-LED display that has served the range since 2021 (and which Apple continues to sell ). Apple calls this a “PROLED XDR Display”. It features individually illuminated pixels, effectively infinite contrast, wonderfully deep blacks and the sort of image quality that Mini-LED, for all its considerable strengths, can’t fully equal. Peak HDR brightness has increased, and Apple has introduced a new display engine designed to manage colour, luminance and pixel usage with extraordinary precision.

Actionable Insight

Apple's new MacBook Pro models feature advanced tandem OLED displays, offering superior contrast and image quality compared to Mini-LED. However, these new displays are reportedly exhibiting a low Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) frequency. This issue can lead to visible flicker, potentially causing discomfort or health problems for sensitive users, despite the display's otherwise high-end specifications.

Community Voice

The community expresses surprise and disappointment that Apple, known for attention to detail, would release a display with such a low PWM frequency, with some speculating it might be higher than reported (e.g., 480hz or 960hz). Many users report significant sensitivity to flicker, experiencing nausea or discomfort from OLED screens, especially at night, and welcome the increased attention to this issue. There's a call for more research into PWM sensitivity, as it appears to affect a notable portion of the population, including those with concussions.
