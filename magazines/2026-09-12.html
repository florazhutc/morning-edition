Shopify Transitions from React Native Back to Native Swift and Kotlin for Mobile Development

Source: original article

Native is now the future of mobile at Shopify (2026) - Shopify Native is now the future of mobile at Shopify Coding agents changed what it costs to build mobile apps twice. Here’s why Shopify is moving from React Native back to Swift and Kotlin. We decided to go all-in on React Native back in 2020, and that bet has been extremely successful.

Actionable Insight

Shopify, after successfully adopting React Native in 2020, is now reverting to native Swift and Kotlin for its mobile applications. This strategic shift is attributed to a change in core assumptions, specifically the impact of large language models (LLMs) on development costs. The company's decision highlights how advancements in AI tools are influencing technology stack choices and potentially making native development more accessible and efficient for large-scale projects.

Community Voice

The community largely validates Shopify's move, with some developers sharing similar experiences of migrating from React Native to native Swift/Kotlin. While some agree that LLMs have altered development cost assumptions, others express skepticism, noting they completed similar migrations without relying on LLMs. Commenters also discuss the initial appeal of React Native for leveraging web developers, contrasting it with the eventual need for dedicated native engineers to achieve platform-specific quality. The scale of Shopify's engineering team, reportedly 3,000 engineers, also sparked discussion.

Actionable Insight

While AI's mathematical problem-solving capabilities have advanced significantly, a declaration argues that the industry's focus on using these achievements as benchmarks is detrimental to the science and community of mathematics. Txt: A New Keyboard-Driven Terminal Text Editor

Source: original article

A fast, keyboard-driven terminal text editor for engineers. Most terminal editors demand a ceremony before you can edit: configuring plugins, learning a modal system, or fighting with a setup that takes hours to get right. GUI editors are the opposite problem — they are powerful, but reaching for the mouse to edit a config file or fix a typo breaks your flow. I built txt because I spend most of my time in the terminal, and switching to a different tool just to make a quick edit always felt like friction I did not want. The editors that live in the terminal either have steep learning curves built around modal paradigms, or they are too limited to be genuinely useful.

Actionable Insight

Txt aims to provide a fast, keyboard-driven terminal text editor designed for engineers, bridging the gap between complex modal editors and GUI-based tools. Its core appeal is reducing friction for quick edits within the terminal, eliminating the need to switch applications for minor tasks. This approach targets users who prioritize staying within their terminal workflow for efficiency.

Community Voice

Community members express strong loyalty to established terminal editors like Vim and Emacs, questioning the need for a new alternative. Several comments highlight a perceived contradiction between the editor's 'keyboard-driven' marketing and its demo, which reportedly features mouse interaction. There's also skepticism regarding the project's stated purpose of quick edits versus its critique of other terminal editors as 'too limited.' Additionally, concerns about security implications of `curl | sh` installation for new, potentially AI-assisted projects are raised, alongside suggestions for comparison with existing tools like `micro-editor`.

Source: original article

A Design Space Exploration of Async/Await A Design Space Exploration of Async/Await Many programming languages now provide the async / await keywords for expressing concurrency. The design rationale is pretty consistent: to make concurrent programs look more like straight-line code (see: Python , Rust , or Swift ). We therefore describe the paradigm which encompasses async/await as straight-line asynchrony , as opposed to using event loops or callbacks.

Actionable Insight

The article systematically analyzes the design choices behind async/await, framing it as 'straight-line asynchrony' to simplify concurrent programming. It contrasts this paradigm with traditional event loops or callbacks, noting its consistent adoption across various languages for making concurrent code appear sequential.
