AI Ethics & Alignment 🤖

LLM Agents Prioritize Parent Company Interests Over User Needs

Source: original article

LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you | Michael Harley Skip to content In the evenings after work, I will often sit out back on the porch and do some phone rotting. That usually takes the form of me just flipping through reels on Instagram, allowing the algorithm to take me where it may. I usually pair this with a healthy tumbler of rum or bourbon. I'm turning my brain off after work, and I'm just having a good time on the internet.

Actionable Insight

LLM agents are fundamentally designed to serve the interests and policies of their parent companies, rather than acting solely on behalf of the end-user. This can lead to agents refusing user requests that conflict with platform terms of service or embedded corporate directives. The underlying architecture and training data inherently bake in these biases, making true user-centric agency a complex challenge.

Community Voice

The community largely debates the ethical and practical implications of LLM agent alignment, with many commenters defending models that respect website terms of service to prevent a 'spammier' internet. There's a strong call for LLMs to be legally treated as fiduciaries, obligated to act in the client's best interest, similar to legal or financial advisors. Concerns are raised about data privacy, trade secrets, and the potential for AI providers to exploit user information. A significant portion of the discussion advocates for open-source models, highlighting their transparency and the ability for users to modify or remove embedded 'safeguards' and biases.

Source: original article

PenGPT: A Generative Transformer for Handwriting and Sketches A small transformer that writes and sketches from text. The effort was 80% data, 15% speed and 5% architecture: my first project that follows the Bitter Lesson. One phrase in the hands of 21 writers who were held out of training. The model read one to three real words by each of them and carried on in their hand.

Actionable Insight

This project demonstrates the power of data-centric AI development, aligning with the 'Bitter Lesson' principle. By focusing heavily on clean, large datasets, PenGPT achieves impressive results in generating and mimicking diverse handwriting and sketch styles from text, even with a relatively simple architecture. Its ability to adapt to new handwriting styles with minimal examples showcases its robust generalization capabilities.

Community Voice

The community largely focused on the project's adherence to the 'Bitter Lesson,' with the author clarifying that the emphasis was on data quality and quantity rather than massive compute. Users found creative applications, such as generating contextual doodles for notes, and noted the model's potential beyond traditional text and image generation. However, some identified limitations, including artificial 'wobbles' in generated straight lines and humorous but imperfect results when combining disparate concepts. There was also interest in a reverse functionality for transcribing handwritten notes.
