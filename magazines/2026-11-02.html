HackerTube: An AI-Powered YouTube Alternative Curating Hacker News-Recommended Videos

Source: original article

It was Friday night at 11pm, I was tired, I went to YouTube to watch something and realized that I was looking at an enshittified wall of slop. Insanely exaggerated thumbnails, dumb recommendations of the same tired topics, truly terrible idiocracy-grade content. I'm sure this algorithm works well for the median person on the internet, but it definitely does not work for me. So this morning (Saturday) I took a hold of my Opus 5.5 and gave it the task: Let's write our own YouTube clone based on whatever videos have been recommended on Hacker News. And Opus came back with: hackertube.org !

Actionable Insight

Born from dissatisfaction with mainstream video platform algorithms, HackerTube demonstrates how AI can be leveraged to create highly specific content feeds based on community preferences. This project highlights a growing user demand for curated, niche content experiences that bypass the 'enshittified' recommendations of large platforms. It showcases the potential for AI to empower individuals to build personalized content discovery tools.

Community Voice

The community largely praised HackerTube for its 'pure nerd content' and the concept of feed disaggregation, suggesting AI could further refine content filtering. Frequent requests included implementing better sorting options, particularly by recency (e.g., last 24 hours, week, month) to address the presence of older content. One user developed an open-source Apple TV app for the service, while another mentioned a similar 'books version.' Concerns were raised about the potential for increased moderation pressure on Hacker News if such projects gain widespread popularity.

Source: Hacker News post

Saw Karpathy's HackerTube and wanted the same thing for books. So I had Claude build one (Opus 5.5). It's ~16.5k books that at least two different HN commenters brought up, from 2007 till now. If your favourite book is missing or matched to the wrong thing, tell me.

Actionable Insight

HackerBooks is an AI-generated platform that compiles approximately 16,500 books frequently mentioned on Hacker News since 2007. This project highlights the growing capability of large language models like Claude to independently develop complex data pipelines and user-facing applications. It offers a unique lens into the literary interests of the HN community, providing downloadable data for further analysis.

Community Voice

The community expresses strong interest in enhanced data access and filtering capabilities, particularly for fiction or cross-referencing with external reviews. Users also noted a perceived decline in book mentions on Hacker News since 2022, speculating on LLM influence. Some raised concerns about data accuracy, such as the reliability of sentiment statistics and missing entries, while generally appreciating the project's utility.

Malicious Backdoor Found in Claude Code VS Code Extension on Cursor Marketplace

Source: original article

Official Claude Code Extension Compromise: Security Advisory | Phorion 🎉 Phorion ranked #1 in independent EDR telemetry evaluations. Phorion has detected a backdoor in the official Claude Code for VS Code extension bundle on the Cursor Marketplace that drops a persistent SSH backdoor across macOS, Linux, and Windows. This write-up focuses primarily on macOS, where the extension attempts to configure an SSH backdoor and establish persistence. Organisations should immediately review their environments for use of Claude Code for VSCode 2.1.312 or 2.1.313 and engage incident response where use of the extension is discovered.

Actionable Insight

A supply chain attack targeted developers using the Claude Code VS Code extension, distributing a malicious version via the Cursor Marketplace that installed a persistent SSH backdoor. This incident underscores the critical security risks associated with third-party software marketplaces and the need for robust verification processes for developer tools. Organizations are advised to review their environments for affected versions and engage incident response.

Community Voice

Community discussion clarified that the incident was a supply chain attack on the Cursor Marketplace, not a direct compromise of Anthropic's systems. Anthropic and Cursor confirmed a malicious actor published a tampered version of the Claude Code extension, which was live for a limited period. Users shared methods to check for the SSH backdoor, which added an attacker's public key to authorized_keys, while also debating the scope of the compromise and the accuracy of initial reports.

Actionable Insight

The 'AI cost' of JavaScript frameworks refers to how their characteristics, such as verbose outputs, complex failure modes, and slow execution, hinder AI's ability to process and interact with them efficiently. I am looking for something in the 60”to 80”range and would like a 4k display with at least 60hz display. I don’t mind paying a premium. I have heard good things about Apple TV as a better alternative to most smart TV native apps. Would some TV’s pair better with this than others for privacy? Actionable Insight

Achieving privacy with modern televisions primarily involves treating them as 'dumb' displays by never connecting them to the internet and relying solely on external streaming devices. While truly privacy-respecting smart TVs are rare, users can mitigate data collection risks through careful setup and, in some cases, hardware modifications.

Community Voice

The community overwhelmingly recommends against connecting any smart TV to the internet, instead advocating for the exclusive use of external streaming devices such as Apple TV, Kodi boxes, or Linux mini-PCs for content. Many users suggest physically isolating the TV from network access, with some even proposing advanced measures like rooting the device to disable telemetry or disconnecting internal Wi-Fi modules. Critical Use-After-Free Vulnerability (CVE-2026-98432) Affects Linux TCP/IPv4 Handshake

Source: original article

Linux's October 2026 TCP Use-After-Free: CVE-2026-98432 — Atlas Trevor Fannin @ trvrio · Founder, Atlas Security alerts and CVE breakdowns, plus infrastructure and general tech. Follow on X (opens in a new tab) Linux's stable kernels shipped a fix on October 11 and 15, 2026 for CVE-2026-98432 , a use-after-free in the IPv4 TCP handshake that a remote, unauthenticated peer can reach. The kernel's own CVE Numbering Authority (CNA) scores it CVSS 3.1 9.8, Critical .

Actionable Insight

A critical use-after-free vulnerability (CVE-2026-98432) has been identified in the Linux kernel's IPv4 TCP handshake, allowing a remote, unauthenticated peer to trigger the flaw. Rated CVSS 9.8, this generic issue is not limited to MPTCP and can be caused by any received IPv4 packet with options. While stable kernels have received fixes, the high severity necessitates prompt patching for affected systems.

Community Voice

Community discussion emphasized that the vulnerability is Linux-specific, not impacting other operating systems like *BSD, Mac, or Windows. While direct internet exploitability was considered unlikely due to many ISP routers dropping packets with IP options, local mitigation by dropping such packets was noted as incomplete. Concerns were raised regarding the absence of immediate patches for major distributions like Ubuntu and Red Hat, though some users suggested that cloud services behind load balancers might offer a degree of protection. The scope of impact beyond servers, including desktop Linux and IoT devices, was also a point of inquiry.
