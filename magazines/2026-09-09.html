Independent Wikis Face Search Visibility Challenges on Google

Source: original article

There’s a new “Google Jail” for independent wikis | Weird Gloop There's a new "Google Jail" for independent wikis We just finished moving the Overwatch and Fortnite wikis off of Fandom. It’s still my favorite thing in the world to help wiki editors grab control of their projects and make them awesome (and not covered with a dozen auto-playing video ads!) Unlike most of the other wikis we host ( runescape.wiki , minecraft.wiki , gta.wiki …), both Overwatch and Fortnite are launching at subdomains of weirdgloop.org instead of on their own root domain, like (say) overwatch.wiki.

Actionable Insight

Independent wikis are migrating from platforms like Fandom due to excessive ads and lack of control, but face significant hurdles in gaining search engine visibility. Google's algorithms appear to struggle with indexing and ranking new, independent domains, often favoring established platforms or requiring specific SEO configurations. This creates a 'Google Jail' effect, making it difficult for high-quality, ad-free alternatives to reach users.

Community Voice

Community members corroborate the difficulty of getting new wikis indexed by Google, even on subdomains of established sites, with some experiencing nearly a year of no search results. Fandom is widely criticized for its ad-heavy and often outdated content, driving users to seek independent alternatives. There's a perception that Google's search heavily favors large, established platforms, hindering the discoverability of smaller, independent projects. Some technical issues like sitemap errors or default Mediawiki settings can also contribute to poor SEO, but the overarching sentiment is that Google's indexing and ranking processes are biased against new, independent content, leading to concerns about content centralization.

New Tool Visualizes LLM Attention Mechanism

Source: original article

One interesting thing about transformer-based large language models are that, during the generation phase, it is able to draw information from any of its previous tokens. But it needs to be selective; if every token affects the generation equally, it won't be very effective. This process needs a mechanism to decide how much a token affects the next token. Turns out, we can visualize this mechanism! You can tap or hover over any of the generated tokens to see the past tokens that affected* the generation.

Actionable Insight

This new tool provides a visual representation of how transformer-based large language models selectively draw information from previous tokens during the generation phase. By illustrating which past tokens influence the generation of the next, it helps demystify the complex attention mechanism. This visualization is crucial for understanding how models decide the relevance and impact of different parts of the input sequence.

Community Voice

The community largely praised the visualization for its clarity and effectiveness in explaining the attention mechanism, with several users noting its utility for teaching and solidifying understanding. Feedback included suggestions for improving the user experience, particularly regarding animation controls, and questions arose about the underlying formulas and the interpretation of 'high influence.' There was also interest in applying similar visualization techniques to image models.

Terence Tao Discusses AI's "Non-Renewable Mining" of Open Math Problems

Source: original article

Terence Tao: "I wrote recently about how the collection of good…" - Mathstodon

Actionable Insight

Mathematician Terence Tao expresses concern that AI's ability to solve open math problems might deplete the pool of challenging questions, hindering future mathematical progress. This "solution-extraction" approach, while effective in the short term, risks undermining the long-term health of the research ecosystem by not fostering deeper understanding or new problem generation. The identification of promising problems, rather than just solving them, is becoming the scarce resource.

Community Voice

The community largely acknowledges Tao's concern, with some agreeing that the true value lies in gaining insight rather than mere problem-solving, suggesting AI's next frontier should be asking challenging questions. Others draw historical parallels to competitive mathematicians or express broader concerns about AI's impact on intellectual creation and the need for copyright. Some speculate that AI labs might be leveraging "mathathons" to gather human research data, while a few believe AI will eventually solve all problems given sufficient hardware.
