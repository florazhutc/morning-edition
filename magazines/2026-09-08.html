Security 🔒

Trusting-Trust Attack Targets Linux Distributions via Binary Manipulation

Source: original article

[2607.24888] Trusting-Trust Attack against an Entire Linux Distribution through Binary Manipulation

Actionable Insight

This research demonstrates that the classic 'Trusting Trust' attack, traditionally associated with compilers, can be extended to compromise entire Linux distributions through binary manipulation. By injecting malicious code into binaries during the build process, an attacker could potentially subvert the entire software supply chain. This highlights a critical vulnerability in the trust chain of widely used operating systems.

Community Voice

The community discussed existing countermeasures, including Wheeler's work and projects like NixOS and Guix, which aim for smaller, verifiable bootstrap seeds or full source bootstraps. Some commenters debated the novelty of the attack, comparing it to ELF executable viruses or noting that Ken Thompson's original paper implied broader applicability beyond compilers. Historical security practices from the TCSEC era, emphasizing traceability and local source builds, were also mentioned as relevant context.

vLLM Explores Speculative Decoding Performance on AMD GPUs

Source: original article

Exploring Speculative Decoding in vLLM on AMD GPUs | vLLM Blog TL;DR: Speculative decoding allows vLLM to verify multiple drafted tokens in a single target-model pass. In our experiments, its effect on output-token throughput varied across drafting methods and proposal lengths, and also depended on the model family, draft checkpoint, workload, and acceptance behavior. Large language models support a wide range of applications, but serving them at scale requires careful optimization. Standard autoregressive decoding is the baseline used by most LLM serving systems: the model generates one token, appends it to the sequence, and then uses the updated sequence to generate the next token.

Actionable Insight

Speculative decoding in vLLM enables the verification of multiple drafted tokens in a single target-model pass, aiming to optimize LLM serving efficiency. Experiments reveal that its effect on output-token throughput is highly variable, depending on factors such as drafting methods, proposal lengths, model family, and acceptance behavior. This variability underscores the nuanced challenges in achieving consistent performance gains across different configurations.

Community Voice

Community discussion highlighted concerns about stock vLLM's performance on workstation-grade AMD GPUs, with some users reporting significantly better throughput from vLLM forks. There was also curiosity regarding the mechanism by which the target model verifies candidate tokens and how acceptance rates on AMD compare to Nvidia GPUs.

Source: original article

It’s still my favorite thing in the world to help wiki editors grab control of their projects and make them awesome (and not covered with a dozen auto-playing video ads!) Unlike most of the other wikis we host ( runescape.wiki , minecraft.wiki , gta.wiki …), both Overwatch and Fortnite are launching at subdomains of weirdgloop.org instead of on their own root domain, like (say) overwatch.wiki.

Actionable Insight

Independent wikis migrating from platforms like Fandom are encountering difficulties with Google's search indexing when hosted on subdomains rather than dedicated root domains. This situation, dubbed 'Google Jail,' suggests that Google's algorithms may be disadvantaging decentralized content, making it harder for these ad-free alternatives to gain visibility. It poses a significant hurdle for creators seeking to provide high-quality, community-driven content outside of large, ad-supported ecosystems.

Community Voice

Hacker News commenters express concern that Google's indexing behavior contributes to the centralization of web content and the rise of 'walled gardens.' Many users share frustration with Fandom's intrusive ads and ad-blocker countermeasures, actively seeking independent alternatives. There's a perceived need for reliable, independent platforms or base domains for wikis, alongside tools like 'Indie Wiki Buddy' to bypass Fandom in search results. Some speculate that Google's financial pressures might be influencing these indexing changes, leading to a decline in search quality for independent content.
