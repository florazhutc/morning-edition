LLMs Like Astra Can Read and Write Neural Network Weights, Improving Models Like Qwen

Source: original article

Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights We find that frontier LLMs are surprisingly good at natively reading and writing neural network weights , well past what we’d expect from humans or conventional algorithms. This might be a big deal for both optimization and interpretability. Just by looking at the raw weights, GPT-6 Astra can tell what a network was trained to do .

Actionable Insight

Frontier LLMs, exemplified by GPT-6 Astra, demonstrate an unexpected ability to natively read and write neural network weights. This capability allows them to understand a network's function and directly edit its parameters to improve performance, such as enhancing a small Qwen model's math skills. This breakthrough could significantly impact AI optimization and interpretability, offering new avenues for model development and understanding.

Community Voice

The community expresses a mix of awe and skepticism, with some calling the capability 'insane' while others caution against overstating the results. Debate centers on the reported '5x improvement' in Qwen's math skills, with one user suggesting the baseline was misrepresented due to a 'thinking mode' where the model looped. There's also discussion on whether this is an emergent capability or a consequence of specific training data and existing research in dynamic weight generation. Some commenters also suggest the paper might conflate distinct findings.

Security 🔒

Ubuntu Delays Patch for Critical Linux Kernel TCP Vulnerability (CVE-2026-98432)

Source: original article

In case you missed it: CVE-2026-98432 is a critical (CVSS 9.8) bug in the Linux kernel's TCP code. It's been there since 2016, so virtually every Linux server on the planet has it, and it can be hit remotely. Anthropic says it built a working exploit that gets root on a server. Debian patched it on October 16 . Ubuntu still doesn't have a patch more than two weeks later.

Actionable Insight

A critical Linux kernel TCP vulnerability, present since 2016 and remotely exploitable, remains unpatched in Ubuntu's default repositories weeks after disclosure and Debian's fix. Despite a CVSS score of 9.8, Ubuntu initially prioritized it as 'High,' leading to community concern over the delay and lack of transparent communication. This situation highlights the tension between rapid patching of severe vulnerabilities and the stability testing required for widespread distribution updates.

Community Voice

The community expresses frustration over Ubuntu's delayed patch for CVE-2026-98432, noting that fixes are only available in 'proposed' repositories and not yet in stable updates. There's significant debate regarding Ubuntu's 'High' priority rating for a 9.8 CVSS vulnerability, with some pointing to a low EPSS score (<1%) suggesting low real-world exploitability, while others cite Anthropic's claim of a root exploit. Canonical's product team has acknowledged the feedback, confirming fixes in -proposed and a published blog post, but users still desire more proactive official communication and clarity on the patching timeline.

Software Development 🐛

PostgreSQL 19 Release Delayed as AI Tools Uncover More Bugs

Source: original article

Robert Haas: Postgres 19 is late, and it's AI's fault Over the past decade, the timing of PostgreSQL major releases has become very predictable: no earlier than the middle of September, and no later than the middle of October. This year is going to be different. PostgreSQL 19 most likely won’t be released until November, and it’s not impossible that the project could decide to postpone the release further. Moreover, this year, several rather large features were reverted quite late in the beta period: UPDATE/DELETE FOR PORTION OF , SQL Property Graph Queries (SQL/PGQ), and ALTER TABLE … MERGE PARTITION/SPLIT PARTITION were all reverted between August 27 and September 15.

Actionable Insight

The PostgreSQL 19 release is significantly delayed, with several major features reverted late in the beta cycle. This postponement is attributed to a substantial increase in bug discovery, largely facilitated by AI-assisted tools. The project faces the challenge of addressing a higher volume of identified issues, impacting its predictable release schedule.

Community Voice

The community largely views AI-assisted bug discovery as a positive development, likening it to 'repaying technical debt' and improving code quality, even in mature codebases. Commenters note AI's strong performance in 'read-only search tasks' like bug identification compared to 'write tasks' such as feature generation. However, a significant challenge highlighted is the need to scale verification and fixing processes, as the increased rate of bug discovery now outpaces the capacity for validation and resolution by maintainers.
