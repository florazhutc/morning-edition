AI in Healthcare 🩺

Specialized CNN Outperforms Frontier LLMs in Skin Cancer Diagnosis

Source: original article

A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis | Lynch Lab Invasive melanoma on the ear, under 0.8 mm thick. Both LLMs called it a seborrhoeic keratosis and said it could be discharged. Clinical photograph on the left, dermoscopic image on the right. Images: MILK10k , MILK study team, CC BY-NC.

Actionable Insight

A decade-old convolutional neural network (CNN) has demonstrated superior accuracy in diagnosing skin cancer compared to leading large language models (LLMs). This highlights that for highly specialized tasks, purpose-built architectures with targeted training data often remain more effective than general-purpose AI models. The finding underscores the continued relevance of specialized AI solutions in critical domains like medical diagnostics.

Community Voice

The community largely agrees that it's not surprising for a specialized model, trained specifically for image recognition in dermatology, to outperform general-purpose LLMs. Many commenters emphasized that LLMs are a tool, not a universal solution, drawing analogies to calculators outperforming LLMs at arithmetic. While acknowledging the CNN's current superiority for this specific task, some also pointed out the potential of LLMs if integrated with broader clinical data like patient notes or interactive questioning, suggesting the comparison might not fully capture LLMs' diagnostic capabilities in a richer context. One comment also referenced 'SkinGPT-4' as an LLM-based model that reportedly does outperform the CNN in some cases.

AI Hardware 🤖

Cerebras Details Deployment of Trillion-Parameter AI Models

Source: original article

How Cerebras Deploys Trillion-Parameter AI Models How we deploy Trillion-parameter frontier models on Cerebras One of the most frequently asked questions we get is how we deploy large models on Cerebras wafer-scale chips. A single Cerebras WSE-3T wafer has 44 GB of on-chip memory, and trillion-parameter frontier models need terabytes for their weights alone. In this post, we’ll explain how we split a large frontier model across multiple WSE-3T wafers, to run multi-trillion parameter models at 1000-2000+ tokens per second.

Actionable Insight

Cerebras has detailed its method for deploying trillion-parameter AI models on its wafer-scale chips, addressing the significant memory requirements. By distributing these large models across multiple WSE-3T wafers, they achieve high-throughput inference, capable of processing 1000-2000+ tokens per second. This strategy allows them to overcome the memory constraints of individual chips for frontier models.

Community Voice

The community expresses skepticism regarding the currency of models available on Cerebras, noting that currently accessible options like GPT OSS 120b or Qwen 3.8 27B are often outdated or inaccurate, despite high token per second (TPS) rates. Commenters suggest Cerebras struggles with customer acquisition due to the high hardware investment required and a perceived lack of competitive, cutting-edge models. There's also criticism directed at the company's team and business execution, despite acknowledging the underlying technology's potential, and concerns about benchmark claims versus actual deployment.

Actionable Insight

This project introduces Architectural Deliberation Records (ADRs) and an ORRCF (Options, Recommendation, Rationale, Confidence, Falsifier) format to structure human-AI collaboration in programming. By versioning these records in Git, it aims to provide oversight for AI-assisted code and scaffold sustainable AI-written solutions. The system is designed to enable AI models to surface doubts and recommend solutions, which humans can then approve or override.

Community Voice

The community generally agrees on the importance of retaining agent interactions but expresses skepticism about the proposed ADR approach. Several users reported negative experiences, citing issues like LLMs generating overly long or mislabeled records, leading to context bloat and degraded code quality. There was also discussion on the optimal placement of ADRs within version control, with some suggesting they belong alongside the code they justify. Some feedback also criticized the project's website for being unclear and verbose.

Actionable Insight

OpenAI and Anthropic have significantly reduced their LLM pricing, with cuts up to 80% within a few months. This aggressive pricing strategy likely reflects intense competition in the rapidly evolving LLM market, driven by new entrants and increasing efficiency. It suggests a shift towards commoditization of foundational models, where cost-effectiveness becomes a critical differentiator.

Community Voice

Hacker News commenters largely attribute the price reductions to fierce competition from alternative LLMs, including cheaper open-source and international models like Kimi, GLM, and DeepSeek. A more cynical view posits that these price cuts, alongside calls for regulation, could be a strategic move to disadvantage competitors, particularly open models from China. Concerns were also raised about the sustainability of such aggressive pricing, with some fearing a 'race to the bottom' or an 'AI winter' for expensive applications.
