Source: Hacker News post

Actionable Insight

LatticeDB is introduced as an embedded graph database designed to offer the local usability and low-latency benefits of SQLite, but optimized for graph data structures. It aims to simplify local development with graph databases, addressing common pain points. Initial benchmarks indicate significant performance gains over SQLite for graph-specific operations like traversals.

Community Voice

Users inquire about production features such as backup solutions (e.g., Litestream integration), concurrent writer handling, and its applicability for mapping RDF data like Wikidata. MS Paint and Photos Invisibly Watermark Locally Generated AI Images with GUIDs

Source: original article

Microsoft Paint and Photos Embed Server-Issued GUIDs as Invisible Watermarks in Locally-Generated Images :: Xusheng Li Microsoft Paint and Photos Embed Server-Issued GUIDs as Invisible Watermarks in Locally-Generated Images Reverse engineering reveals how Paint and Photos embed a server-issued GUID into the pixels of locally generated AI images. Microsoft Paint supports both local and cloud image generation Paint and Photos also ship local AI models

Actionable Insight

Microsoft's Paint and Photos applications are embedding invisible, server-issued GUIDs into the pixels of AI-generated images, even those created using local models. This practice, revealed through reverse engineering, raises significant privacy concerns as it silently tags user-generated content without explicit consent. The watermarks could potentially link images back to individual users, regardless of whether the content was generated locally or via cloud services.

Community Voice

The community expresses strong disapproval, labeling the practice a "massive privacy overreach" due to the injection of hidden, unique identifiers without user consent. Many commenters argue that the AI aspect is a "red herring," emphasizing that the core issue is Microsoft's silent tagging of user-generated content, which could enable linking images to personal information via subpoenas. Concerns extend to the potential for other local tools to employ similar tactics and highlight a broader distrust of Microsoft's customer-unfriendly practices, with some users reporting incorrect AI watermark triggers even for basic image edits.
