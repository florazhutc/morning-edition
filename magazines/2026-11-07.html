Special Character in Unix `/etc/passwd` Leads to `musl-libc` Vulnerability

Source: original article

back in old unix your password hash just sat in /etc/passwd , right next to your username. in 1980 bill joy put a * there for an account called daemon . nobody's supposed to ever log in as it. a * isn't a hash, so no password can match it. Address github.com/dspinellis/unix-history-repo/blob/BSD-3/etc/passwd

Actionable Insight

The `*` character in `/etc/passwd`, originally intended to disable logins for accounts like `daemon` by not being a valid hash, can be exploited due to "in-band signaling." This vulnerability arises when `crypt` functions, particularly in `musl-libc`, mishandle this special character, allowing an attacker to bypass password validation. It underscores the risks of using "magic strings" instead of explicit error handling in system-level programming.

Community Voice

Hacker News commenters identified the vulnerability as an "in-band signaling" issue, where the `*` character, meant to signify "no password," is misinterpreted by `crypt` functions, especially in `musl-libc`. This oversight in `musl-libc` drew criticism, as a similar workaround for a different case was present but missed this specific scenario. The discussion highlighted the dangers of "magic strings" in system programming and questioned the assumption that long-standing software is inherently more secure, noting that "cruft" can accumulate over time. The vulnerability was reproduced on Alpine Linux, though `PermitRootLogin` settings offer some mitigation.

Ubuntu 26.04's Rust-based sudo introduces compatibility issues with standard sudo configurations

Source: original article

You're probably reading this page because you've attempted to access some part of my blog (Wandering Thoughts) or CSpace , the wiki thing it's part of. Unfortunately you're using a browser version that my anti-crawler precautions consider suspicious, most often because it's too old (most often this applies to versions of Chrome). Unfortunately, as of early 2025 there's a plague of high volume crawlers (apparently in part to gather data for LLM training) that use a variety of old browser user agents, especially Chrome user agents. To reduce the load on Wandering Thoughts I'm experimenting with (attempting to) block all of them, and you've run into this. If this is in error and you're using a current version of your browser of choice, you can contact me at my current place at the university (you should be able to work out the email address from that).

Actionable Insight

The adoption of a Rust-based `sudo` in Ubuntu 26.04 is causing compatibility problems, particularly with `sudoers` glob expressions. This change is seen by some as a necessary evolution of system tools, while others express frustration over breaking changes and increasing incompatibility across Linux distributions. The shift highlights a tension between modernizing core utilities and maintaining backward compatibility for system administrators.

Community Voice

The community response indicates significant frustration, with some users citing this as further validation for migrating to Debian. There's a debate about whether tools should evolve, even if it means breaking changes, versus the need for compatibility. Specific concerns include the removal of certain `sudoers` glob expressions, which some deem a 'footgun' and easy to fix, while others criticize the new `sudo` for retaining dangerous wildcards while removing specific ones. Broader concerns about declining compatibility between Linux distros and the Linux Foundation's focus are also raised.
