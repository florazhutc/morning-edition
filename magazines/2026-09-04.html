Third-Level .name Domains to Be Terminated in 2026

Source: original article

2026 .name Termination Blockly AGC 2025 2024 2023 2022 2021 2020 2019 2018 2017 2016 2015 2014 2013 2012 2011 2010 2009 2008 2007 2006 2005 2004 2003 2002 Nearly twenty-five years ago I registered neil.fraser.name to provide a stable presence on the Internet. It has been the home of this website, my email address, and a server for APIs. It predates YouTube, Facebook, and smart phones. Minutes after my daughter was born, I also registered beverly.fraser.name .

Actionable Insight

The upcoming termination of third-level .name domains, such as x.y.name, by 2026 threatens long-established personal internet presences. This decision impacts users who registered these domains for stable online identities, some nearly twenty-five years ago. It raises questions about the long-term reliability of domain name services and the stability of internet identifiers.

Community Voice

The community expresses strong disapproval, arguing that existing third-level .name registrations should be honored rather than terminated, even if new registrations cease. Many commenters believe this action directly contradicts ICANN's mission to ensure stable and secure internet identifier systems. There is also criticism of the .name TLD's structure itself, with some hoping for legal intervention, similar to past issues with the .org TLD, to prevent the termination of these long-held domains.

History & Data 📜

"Any Human Ever" Generates Random Historical Lives from Over 100 Billion People

Source: original article

Any Human Ever: one life from over 100 billion Over 100,000,000,000 people have ever lived. You'll draw, step by step: a year, a place, a life, each taken at random from real data . Almost everyone who ever lived was born recently, because population grew exponentially, so a random birth is far more likely to fall near the present.

Actionable Insight

The "Any Human Ever" project generates a random life from over 100 billion people who have ever lived, drawing on real data for year, place, and life details. While aiming to illustrate historical demographics, the exponential growth of the human population means a truly random selection would heavily favor recent births, a point the project acknowledges. However, the accuracy and contextual relevance of historical data, especially for ancient times, pose significant challenges to such simulations.

Community Voice

Users found the concept intriguing, sharing examples of ancient lives generated, often highlighting the limited travel common in antiquity. However, significant concerns were raised regarding the accuracy and consistency of the historical data presented, particularly for marriage statistics and modern monetary comparisons for ancient economies. Some questioned the randomness distribution, noting a prevalence of ancient lives despite the stated likelihood of modern births. Despite these data integrity issues, the project was appreciated for its potential as a creative tool, such as for role-playing games, and for prompting reflection on human history.
