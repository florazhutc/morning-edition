Apple macOS Developer Tools Remain Vulnerable to Compromised eMudhra Certificates

Source: original article

Apple's git still trusts the compromised eMudhra certificates Apple's git still trusts the compromised eMudhra certificates Apple was notified of the eMudhra incident on 17th October. As of 28th October, the developer tools Apple ships with macOS, including git, curl and python3, still trust the compromised certificates. Worse, macOS 27.2, released on 26th October, did not fix the issue and undid the workaround that users had applied by hand, leaving them vulnerable again.

Actionable Insight

Apple's macOS developer tools, including git, curl, and python3, continue to trust compromised eMudhra certificates despite prior notification. A recent macOS update not only failed to address the vulnerability but also reverted user-applied workarounds, leaving systems exposed. This incident highlights a critical flaw in Apple's certificate management and patch deployment for essential security issues.

Community Voice

The community notes that the issue extends beyond git to various system-provided utilities, indicating a broader macOS-level certificate trust problem. Many users express frustration with Apple's slow response and the lack of a mechanism for small security patches outside of major OS updates, drawing comparisons to Windows Update. There's also discussion about the perceived abandonment of certificate revocation lists (CRLs) and OCSP, and suggestions for macOS to route command-line tools through its robust `Security.framework/trustd` for better certificate validation. Apple has since provided a recommended patch for developers to apply manually.

Python's Experimental JIT Compiler Work Paused in CPython

Source: original article

Python's experimental JIT to be removed [LWN.net] If you enjoy this article, please consider subscribing to LWN . Thank you for visiting LWN.net! Python's experimental just-in-time (JIT) compiler , which was added to CPython in 2024 for the 3.13 release , is on its way out. In June, the Python steering council announced that it was pausing work on the JIT in CPython's main branch until there was an accepted Python Enhancement Proposal (PEP) that addressed concerns about maintenance, compatibility, and set clear success metrics as well as a timeline for completion.

Actionable Insight

Python's experimental just-in-time (JIT) compiler, initially added for the CPython 3.13 release, has been put on hold by the steering council. This decision was made due to unresolved concerns regarding its long-term maintenance, compatibility, and the lack of clear success metrics and a defined timeline. Work on the JIT will not resume in CPython's main branch until an accepted Python Enhancement Proposal addresses these critical issues.

Community Voice

The community expresses disappointment over the pause of the JIT, with some attributing it to bureaucracy and a lack of developer resources. Many commenters suggest existing performance alternatives such as Cython, Numba, PyPy, or GraalPy. While some defend the decision as prudent given the maintenance and compatibility concerns, others highlight the perceived difficulty of implementing significant changes within the CPython core.
