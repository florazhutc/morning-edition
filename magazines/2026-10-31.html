Mistral Releases Open-Weight 1-Trillion-Parameter Large 4 Model with Enhanced Coding and Cybersecurity

Source: original article

Mistral Large 4 (Le Chonk) is now open weight | Mistral Mistral Large 4 (Le Chonk) Built for Coders, Trusted by Defenders. Mistral released Mistral Large 4 (Le Chonk), a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model, now available with improved coding, cybersecurity, and reasoning efficiency. It leads open-weight benchmarks in coding and defensive security while offering three reasoning modes for cost-performance balance. Fully open-weight and European-trained, it empowers sovereign AI with customizable, self-hosted deployment options.

Actionable Insight

Mistral has launched Mistral Large 4, a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model, now fully open-weight and featuring significant improvements in coding, cybersecurity, and reasoning efficiency. This European-trained model leads open-weight benchmarks in key areas and offers flexible reasoning modes for cost-performance optimization. Its release targets sovereign AI initiatives with customizable, self-hosted deployment options.

Community Voice

The community notes a substantial price reduction for Mistral Large 4 compared to its preview, which is seen as crucial for its competitiveness. Users express strong support for Mistral as a European provider, particularly for EU-based companies, and welcome the increased competition in the AI market. While the model's high refusal rate for malicious cyber requests is highlighted, some question the transparency of related performance metrics. Discussions also cover the significant hardware demands for self-hosting such large models, often requiring quantization, and specific licensing requirements for Model-as-a-Service businesses.

Backdoored Claude Code VS Code Extension Published on Cursor Marketplace

Source: original article

Official Claude Code Extension Compromise: Security Advisory | Phorion 🎉 Phorion ranked #1 in independent EDR telemetry evaluations. Phorion has detected a backdoor in the official Claude Code for VS Code extension bundle on the Cursor Marketplace that drops a persistent SSH backdoor across macOS, Linux, and Windows. This write-up focuses primarily on macOS, where the extension attempts to configure an SSH backdoor and establish persistence. Organisations should immediately review their environments for use of Claude Code for VSCode 2.1.312 or 2.1.313 and engage incident response where use of the extension is discovered.

Actionable Insight

A malicious actor exploited access to the Cursor Marketplace to distribute a backdoored version of the Claude Code VS Code extension, not compromising Anthropic's systems directly. This tampered extension installed a persistent SSH backdoor across macOS, Linux, and Windows, posing a significant supply chain security risk for developers. The incident underscores the critical need for vigilance regarding extensions from third-party marketplaces and robust security practices in the software supply chain.

Community Voice

Hacker News comments clarified that Anthropic's systems were not compromised; instead, a malicious actor used access to the Cursor Marketplace to publish a tampered version of the Claude Code extension. Users shared specific steps to check for the SSH backdoor, which added a public key labeled `svc-telemetry` to authorized keys. The discussion also addressed initial confusion regarding the extension's origin and the specific marketplaces affected, with some noting the hyperbolic nature of early reports and the limited timeframe the malicious version was active.

AI's Impact on SRE: Deskilling Concerns Emerge

Source: original article

When AI handles 95% of your incident response, do you get worse at handling the 5% that actually matters? & This piece took 6 days, 5 hours to be cooked, hope we served. There are two popular prophecies floating around tech circles these days. The first says SRE is the future of all software engineering , that as AI writes more and more code, the humans who remain will be the ones keeping systems alive. The second says AI will devour every tech job alive, SREs included.

Actionable Insight

The increasing reliance on AI for routine incident response raises concerns that Site Reliability Engineers (SREs) may lose critical skills needed for the most complex and non-routine issues. While AI can handle a significant portion of incidents, over-automation could impair human ability to manage the challenging 5% that truly matter. This potential deskilling challenges the long-term effectiveness and core responsibilities of the SRE role.

Community Voice

The community largely acknowledges the deskilling concern, frequently drawing parallels to the aviation industry where pilots maintain manual flying skills despite advanced autopilots. Some commenters view AI as a 'superhuman' tool that can accelerate learning and efficiency, similar to how tools like Terraform previously 'deskilled' certain tasks but ultimately improved processes. There's a sentiment that SRE's core value lies in deep system understanding, which AI could either augment or, if over-relied upon, diminish. A few voices also question the fundamental nature of the SRE role itself.

Actionable Insight

This project demonstrates a novel method of embedding a complete Nintendo Entertainment System CPU and RAM directly into the weights of a Qwen LLM, building on the Qwenputer concept. It leverages the model's Gated DeltaNet state to function as the machine's memory. While a significant technical achievement, this approach highlights the extreme inefficiency of LLMs for deterministic computation, running hundreds of times slower than traditional emulation.

Community Voice

The community finds this project a fascinating, if inefficient, demonstration of compiling complex systems into LLM weights, with some seeing potential for embedding small VMs or interpreters within LLMs. Many acknowledge it as a 'hack' that underscores the massive computational overhead of LLMs compared to deterministic programs, noting the significant performance penalty. The technical ingenuity of simulating both CPU and RAM within the model's weights is appreciated, though some question the practical utility of such an approach.

AI/ML 🧠

Sleep Framework Enhances LLM Knowledge Retention in Continual Learning

Source: original article

The sleep framework: A new way for LLMs to retain knowledge in continual learning

Actionable Insight

The 'sleep framework' proposes a novel method for LLMs to consolidate knowledge during continual learning, leveraging techniques like on-policy distillation and parameter expansion. This approach aims to prevent catastrophic forgetting by selectively integrating new memories without overwriting previously learned information. Its effectiveness and scalability to very large models are key areas of interest.

Community Voice

The community praises the article's clarity in explaining technical concepts, with some noting similarities to Meta's Muse and DreamCoder. While viewed as a crucial step towards AGI, concerns are raised regarding the framework's scalability to much larger models, as experiments were conducted on comparatively smaller ones. The efficiency of parameter expansion and the lack of published data on parameter storage (e.g., system RAM, SSD) are also points of discussion.
