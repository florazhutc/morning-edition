Eight eMudhra Intermediate CA Private Keys Compromised

Source: original article

The private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates have been compromised. They chain to roots trusted by the Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple and Chrome root programs and by many downstream trust stores. To demonstrate that the keys are compromised, the original reporter Wayne signed the following message with the private key of each of the eight intermediate CAs and reported it to the Root Program Teams on the 17th of October: proof.zip contains the message, and for each intermediate the certificate, its public key and a signature on the message. openssl x509 -in EM-DV-TLS-CA-G3B.crt.pem -noout -pubkey | diff - EM-DV-TLS-CA-G3B.pub.pem openssl dgst -sha384 -verify EM-DV-TLS-CA-G3B.pub.pem -signature EM-DV-TLS-CA-G3B.sig message.txt

Actionable Insight

The private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates, trusted by major root programs, have been compromised, posing a significant security risk. Reports indicate some private keys were trivially weak, potentially due to a flaw in the key generation script. This incident highlights critical vulnerabilities in certificate authority key management practices.

Community Voice

The community expresses a mixed sense of urgency regarding the compromise, with some noting the extreme weakness of the private keys (e.g., a single byte) and a flaw in the key generation script as the likely cause. While major browsers have rapid remediation tools, concerns are raised about the difficulty of updating embedded CA stores in other software. There are also allegations that the keys were publicly accessible and reports of them being used to issue a large number of certificates for domains like google.com.

Claim: C# Outperforms Rust in WASM Size, But Community Raises Concerns

Source: original article

GitHub - zion-sati/NetWasm: Compile C# and .NET CIL to small standalone Wasm with WASI (AOT with built-in Runtime and Precise GC) · GitHub You signed in with another tab or window. Reload to refresh your session. You signed out in another tab or window. Reload to refresh your session.

Actionable Insight

A new project, NetWasm, claims C# can produce smaller WebAssembly binaries than Rust, leveraging AOT compilation with a built-in runtime and precise garbage collection. This claim is met with skepticism, as critics point to potential unfair comparisons, such as post-processing of C# code not applied to other targets, and Rust's self-managed memory versus C#'s reliance on a garbage collector. The project's non-open-source status also raises considerations for developers evaluating the technology.

Community Voice

The community largely disputes the claim that C# consistently beats Rust in WASM size, citing an unfair comparison where the C# code is post-processed and includes a built-in runtime/GC, unlike the Rust benchmarks. Some argue Rust binaries can be significantly smaller, or suggest Zig as a superior alternative for WASM size and performance. Concerns were also raised about the non-open-source nature of the `NetWasm` project, and some commenters questioned the overall relevance and appeal of WebAssembly for general application development.
