Email Security 📧

Spammers Adopt ASCII Smuggling to Evade Email Filters

Source: original article

Once popular for attacking AI, ASCII smuggling is embraced by spammers - Ars Technica Size Small Standard Large Width * Standard Wide Links Standard Orange A clever technique used to hide malicious prompts in attacks on AI agents has been adopted by spammers to evade filters on email platforms that are designed to flag unwanted messages used in mass campaigns. The technique is broadly known as ASCII smuggling. It gained attention two years ago as a means of making a class of AI attack known as prompt injections more stealthy.

Actionable Insight

ASCII smuggling, a technique previously used to hide malicious prompts in AI attacks, has now been adopted by spammers. This method allows them to bypass email platform filters designed to detect and flag unwanted messages. Its emergence presents a new challenge for email providers in combating sophisticated spam campaigns.

Community Voice

The community expresses significant frustration with the general ineffectiveness of current spam filters, particularly those from Microsoft, which some users accuse of being a spamming platform themselves. Commenters suggest technical solutions like text sanitization and discuss broader issues of unwanted email beyond traditional spam, including platform notifications and accidental sign-ups, leading some to adopt personal email proxy strategies.
