WhatCable: Mac Menu Bar App Instantly Unmasks USB-C Cable Power, Speed, and Display Support

USB-C cables can be a mess. One cable charges at 5W, another does 100W and Thunderbolt 4, and they look identical in the drawer. WhatCable sits in your menu bar and reads the cable data your Mac already has access to. Plug in a cable and it tells you in plain English what it can actually do: charging wattage, data speed, display support, Thunderbolt, etc. Built in Swift/SwiftUI. Open source, free, no tracking. GitHub: https://github.com/darrylmorley/whatcable

Actionable Insight

WhatCable provides a much-needed solution to the common frustration of indistinguishable yet vastly different USB-C cables, leveraging existing macOS data to demystify their capabilities. This simple, open-source utility empowers users to make informed choices, preventing issues like slow charging or display incompatibilities by surfacing crucial information in plain English. Its existence underscores the growing complexity of modern hardware standards and the demand for accessible diagnostic tools.

Community Voice

The community expresses strong positive sentiment towards WhatCable, praising its utility as a native Mac app and highlighting the developer's exceptional responsiveness to feedback, evidenced by rapid updates and the quick inclusion of CLI and regular app options. While there are some reports of initial detection issues for a few users, there's also significant enthusiasm and demand for similar functionality or direct ports for Linux.

Credit Cards Are Vulnerable To Brute Force Kind Attacks | Metin's Blog Credit Cards Are Vulnerable To Brute Force Kind Attacks These days, storing and showing what's visible on UI's and receipts are highly standardized and regulated by the industry standards such as PCI DSS. And even when you save your card on an ecommerce website, which strictly follows the PCI DSS, your card can still be stolen - or derived. PCI DSS is the widely known and implemented industry standard for defining bare-minimum security measures that should be taken when handling sensitive banking data such as credit cards.

Actionable Insight

This article exposes a critical vulnerability where credit cards are susceptible to brute-force attacks, even when systems comply with the widely adopted PCI DSS standard. It highlights a concerning gap in current financial security protocols, suggesting that adherence to industry regulations alone does not guarantee imperviousness to sophisticated or simple attack vectors.

Community Voice

The community largely disputes the idea of direct brute-force attacks on credit card numbers, stating that payment processors actively prevent such enumeration. However, they acknowledge that "card testing" or "probing" via small charges is a real and common issue, often exploiting weak merchant security or a lack of consistent 3D Secure adoption. While consumers are generally protected by chargebacks, there's widespread frustration over the payment system's reliance on outdated security measures.
