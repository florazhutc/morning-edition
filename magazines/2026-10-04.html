Court Sides with EFF, Declares Utah's VPN Law Technically Impossible

Source: original article

Court Agrees with EFF: Utah’s VPN Law Demands a Technical Impossibility | Electronic Frontier Foundation Skip to main content Email updates on news, actions, and events in your area. If you use technology, this fight is yours. EFFecting Change: Protect the Vote on October 8 Court Agrees with EFF: Utah’s VPN Law Demands a Technical Impossibility

Actionable Insight

A recent court ruling affirmed the Electronic Frontier Foundation's stance that Utah's VPN law, which aims to block adult content, demands a technical impossibility. The law effectively requires platforms to either block all VPN traffic nationwide or withdraw services from Utah entirely. This decision underscores the challenges governments face in regulating internet content without a clear understanding of network technology.

Community Voice

The community questions the feasibility of reliably detecting VPN traffic, noting that any proxy can obscure a connection. There's skepticism about the truism that 'the internet will always route around censorship,' with some pointing to nations like China and Iran as examples where censorship has been effective. Concerns are raised about the broader implications of such laws, viewing them as potential steps towards increased government control and First Amendment violations, with one user drawing parallels to Russia's approach to foreign traffic. Some also question why simpler domain-based filtering isn't considered, and others note that lawmakers often disregard technical impossibilities.

Aleph Alpha Releases Kolibri, an Open-Weight English-German MoE Model with 1M Token Context

Source: original article

Kolibri Has Landed: A Sovereign Open-Weight Model — Aleph Alpha Kolibri Has Landed: A Sovereign Open-Weight Model On the Day of German Reunification, we are releasing our new model: Kolibri. Kolibri is an English-German Mixture-of-Experts Transformer with 78B total parameters, 3B active. It supports context lengths of up to 1M tokens.

Actionable Insight

Aleph Alpha has launched Kolibri, an open-weight English-German Mixture-of-Experts Transformer featuring 78B total parameters (3B active) and supporting context lengths up to 1M tokens. A notable aspect is its training with abstention data and the Merlin-Arthur protocol, enabling it to explicitly state 'I don't know' when an answer is not in context, aiming to reduce hallucinations. The release emphasizes a high degree of transparency in its development and dataset creation.

Community Voice

The community largely commends Aleph Alpha for the unprecedented level of openness in their technical report, detailing the model's creation and dataset. However, the 'sovereign' claim is a point of contention, with discussions arising about its validity given the company's impending merger with Canadian firm Cohere. Some users also question the benchmarking choices, noting the absence of comparisons to more recent MoE models and pointing out that other models reportedly outperform Kolibri in certain German benchmarks. The model's ability to abstain from answering when uncertain is highlighted as a positive feature.

💰 Cost Management

Hard Budget Caps Advocated for Pay-by-Usage Services

Source: original article

We’re going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything We’re going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything Here’s a product feature which the world is going to need a whole lot more of over the coming months and years: default hard budget caps . I’m talking about the feature of pay-by-usage services and APIs that lets you say “after $X/month, cut this thing off and return errors”. Soft caps, “after $X/month, send me a warning email”, will not cut it.

Actionable Insight

The article emphasizes the urgent need for default hard budget caps on pay-by-usage services and APIs. These caps would automatically terminate service and return errors upon reaching a predefined spending limit, rather than merely issuing warnings. This feature is presented as crucial for preventing unexpected cost overruns for users.

Community Voice

The community largely supports the idea of hard budget caps, expressing surprise at their general absence in cloud providers and speculating that this omission might be a deliberate strategy to encourage overspending. However, some commenters also highlight the operational difficulties and potential customer backlash from abrupt service cut-offs, especially during periods of organic growth. There's also discussion about extending the concept of hard limits beyond financial costs to other system resources for overall reliability.

Actionable Insight

A federal judge has characterized Flock license plate readers as 'indiscriminate mass surveillance,' raising questions about the legality and constitutionality of such systems. This ruling challenges the common legal stance that individuals have no expectation of privacy in public. The tension between public safety and privacy rights remains a central issue, especially when the technology proves effective in crime fighting.

Community Voice

The Hacker News community discussed whether license plate readers should be more targeted rather than a 'dragnet,' and debated if they violate privacy expectations in public, given past court rulings. While acknowledging instances where the technology aided in discovering illegal substances, many expressed concerns about the progression towards 'total surveillance' and drew comparisons to 'Minority Report.' Some contrasted Flock's approach with tech companies like Google and Apple, which have reportedly shifted towards on-device location history storage to avoid legal issues.
