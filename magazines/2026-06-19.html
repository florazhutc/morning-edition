🎓 Education

Cornell's Advanced Compilers (CS 6120): A PhD-Level Self-Guided Online Course

CS 6120: Advanced Compilers: The Self-Guided Online Course CS 6120 is a PhD-level Cornell CS course by Adrian Sampson on programming language implementation. It covers universal compilers topics like intermediate representations, data flow, and “classic” optimizations as well as more research-flavored topics such as parallelization, just-in-time compilation, and garbage collection. The work consists of reading papers and open-source hacking tasks, which use LLVM and an educational IR invented just for this class . This page lists the curriculum for following this course at the university of your imagination, for four imagination credits (ungraded).

Actionable Insight

This online, self-guided Cornell course democratizes access to advanced compiler design, blending foundational theory with cutting-edge research topics like JIT compilation and parallelization. Its strong pedagogical approach, combining academic papers with practical LLVM-based "hacking" tasks, offers an invaluable resource for both aspiring academics and industry professionals looking to deepen their understanding of programming language implementation.

Community Voice

The community largely questions whether the "Advanced Compilers" course truly lives up to its title, with many topics appearing more foundational than advanced. There's also specific technical criticism regarding the dynamic compilers section, suggesting it overemphasizes "trace compilation," which is considered a dead end, instead of more relevant modern concepts. This suggests a desire for a course that delves deeper into contemporary and cutting-edge compiler design.

They are all from different contributors, have different names, and are not forks of other repositories. But they share a common pattern, which is what allowed me to write a script to find such repositories.

Actionable Insight

This discovery of 10,000 unique GitHub repositories distributing Trojan malware highlights a massive and sophisticated supply-chain attack vector. It underscores the growing challenge for platforms like GitHub in proactively detecting widespread, polymorphic malicious content that leverages legitimate hosting for broad distribution. The unique accounts and repository structures suggest an automated, well-resourced operation targeting developers and users who download code, posing a significant threat to software integrity.

Community Voice

The community widely agrees that GitHub is experiencing a pervasive and sophisticated problem with malware distribution, characterized by malicious actors cloning new or popular projects and employing tactics like frequent updates to game search rankings. Many users have personally encountered such incidents, often involving derivatives of their own projects. There is a strong consensus that GitHub is failing to adequately address this widespread issue, showing a lack of effective moderation or concern despite clear reports of malicious activity.

You are in charge of drawing the district lines for the elections. Click adjacent tiles on the map to group them into a district. Each district must be one connected shape, no islands allowed. Coloured houses show where voters live. Not every tile on the board has one, some are just empty land.

Actionable Insight

This 'Show HN' brilliantly transforms the complex and often controversial political practice of gerrymandering into an accessible and engaging daily puzzle game. By having players redraw electoral districts, it offers an intuitive, hands-on understanding of how district lines can manipulate election outcomes. This innovative approach could serve as a unique educational tool, subtly raising awareness about the impact of electoral design.

Community Voice
