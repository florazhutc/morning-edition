Actionable Insight

This incident sharply illustrates the tension between employee innovation, even when beneficial and widely adopted, and stringent corporate intellectual property policies. 💰 AI Business

AI's Costly Bait-and-Switch: Free Access Now Means Expensive Addiction Later

A year ago in The Back Of The AI Envelope I pointed out that the AI platforms were running the drug-dealer's algorithm, "the first one's free". By massively subsidizing the use of their products, they were generating overwhelming demand for them. They used this demand to justify massive investments, in the hope that, by the time they had to show a return on these invetment, the users would be so addicted that they would pay the vastly higher prices needed to generate a return. I have to confess that I was late to the party. The earliest skepticism I've been able to find was from Sequoia Capital's David Cahn in September 2023, entitled AI’s $200B Question .

Actionable Insight

This piece critically examines the potentially unsustainable "drug-dealer's algorithm" employed by AI platforms, where initial subsidization creates artificial demand to justify massive investments. It raises crucial questions about the long-term financial viability of current AI investment trends, suggesting an impending reckoning where users must pay significantly higher prices or the market could face a significant correction.

Community Voice

The Hacker News community largely believes that while AI services are currently expensive, their costs are expected to decrease over time as the technology matures and competition, especially from open-source models, intensifies. However, a more significant concern is the struggle companies face in achieving a clear return on investment (ROI) from AI implementations, leading to strategic re-evaluations. Many anticipate that upcoming IPOs will provide much-needed transparency into the true unit economics.

Mistral OCR 4 : SOTA OCR for Document Intelligence Today, we're releasing Mistral OCR 4, featuring bounding boxes, block classification, and inline confidence scores alongside extracted text. The model supports 170 languages across 10 language groups, runs in a single container for fully self-hosted deployments, and serves as an ingestion component for enterprise search, RAG, and domain-specific retrieval pipelines .
