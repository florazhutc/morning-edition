Actionable Insight

A new proof reportedly disconfirms the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), marking a surprising and rare development in complexity theory. While significant, this result does not immediately imply that general NP problems are subexponential or directly resolve the P vs. NP problem. Notably, another research lab, Anthropic, has also reportedly released a similar proof using a different methodology.

Community Voice

The community expresses surprise at the disproof of ETH, noting the rarity of such complexity class separations. Commenters clarify that the result does not directly imply NP is subexponential or have immediate bearing on P vs. NP, as runtime bounds do not generally transfer under polynomial-time reductions. There is discussion about Anthropic also claiming a disproof of ETH with a different approach, raising questions about potential immediate applications for SAT solving and implications for prior proofs that relied on the Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH). Some speculate on the long-term sustainability of frontier labs dedicating significant compute to such theoretical proofs and potential implications for counting problems like 2SAT.
