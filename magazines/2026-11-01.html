Malicious Claude Code VS Code Extension on Cursor Marketplace Drops SSH Backdoor

Source: original article

Official Claude Code Extension Compromise: Security Advisory | Phorion 🎉 Phorion ranked #1 in independent EDR telemetry evaluations. Phorion has detected a backdoor in the official Claude Code for VS Code extension bundle on the Cursor Marketplace that drops a persistent SSH backdoor across macOS, Linux, and Windows. This write-up focuses primarily on macOS, where the extension attempts to configure an SSH backdoor and establish persistence. Organisations should immediately review their environments for use of Claude Code for VSCode 2.1.312 or 2.1.313 and engage incident response where use of the extension is discovered.

Actionable Insight

A tampered version of the Claude Code VS Code extension was distributed via the Cursor Marketplace, installing a persistent SSH backdoor on macOS, Linux, and Windows systems. This incident was not a compromise of Anthropic's internal systems but rather an exploitation of the third-party Cursor Marketplace by a malicious actor. It highlights critical supply chain security vulnerabilities inherent in software extension ecosystems.

Community Voice

Hacker News comments clarified that the incident stemmed from a malicious actor publishing a tampered extension on the Cursor Marketplace, rather than a compromise of Anthropic's systems. Users shared specific instructions for checking if their SSH authorized keys were impacted. Discussions also touched upon the distinction between various extension registries, the timeline of the malicious extension's availability, and broader concerns regarding extension auto-update mechanisms and the definition of 'trusted publishers' in VS Code.

NES Contra ASM to C to WebAssembly Port Adds Online Multiplayer

Source: Hacker News post

Based on vermiceli's annotated disassembly of Contra: https://github.com/vermiceli/nes-contra-us . Thanks for the great work. I ported it in two steps. First to C: the game logic is translated routine by routine from the 6502 assembly, and checked frame by frame against the original in the Mesen emulator. Then the C code to WebAssembly so it runs in the browser. The cherry on top: online multiplayer, so two players can play co-op over the internet. Source: https://github.com/epoyraz/nes-contra-us

Actionable Insight

This project meticulously ports the 1987 NES game Contra from 6502 assembly to C, then to WebAssembly, ensuring frame-by-frame fidelity to the original. This technical feat enables browser-based gameplay and introduces a new online multiplayer feature. The process highlights the challenges and precision required in translating low-level assembly code while preserving game logic.

Community Voice

The community lauded the extensive effort involved, particularly the detailed original 6502 assembly annotations. Users expressed interest in the porting process and architectural choices, while also pointing out issues such as missing audio and incorrect keyboard button mapping. Broader discussions touched on the potential impact of AI usage restrictions on such 'labor of love' projects and the general techniques of transcompilation for retro games.

Binance Suffers $6.3 Billion Crypto Heist Executed in 20 Minutes

Source: original article

The $6.3 Billion Binance Hack Was Pulled Off in Just 20 Minutes | WIRED Cryptocurrency firms and investigators are racing to freeze and track down billions of dollars of stolen digital cash following what appears to be the largest crypto heist of all time. On Thursday, starting at around 4pm ET, hackers started quickly siphoning around $6.3 billion in cryptocurrencies, spread across multiple blockchains, from wallets belonging to crypto exchange Binance . Following the theft, Binance almost immediately suspended all deposits and withdrawals from its services as part of its investigation into the hack and says it is working to identify who was behind the breach. The company, along with other crypto firms, says it has so far been able to freeze around half of the stolen funds—essentially stopping hackers moving the money further or trying to cash it out.

Actionable Insight

A massive $6.3 billion cryptocurrency heist targeted Binance, with hackers siphoning funds across multiple blockchains in just 20 minutes. Binance swiftly suspended services and, in collaboration with other crypto firms, managed to freeze approximately half of the stolen assets. This incident underscores both the speed with which large-scale crypto thefts can occur and the industry's capacity for rapid, coordinated recovery efforts.

Community Voice

Community discussion questions Binance's security practices, suggesting potential negligence given the scale and speed of the theft. Commenters emphasize that while the execution was quick, the preparation likely spanned years. There is speculation regarding the specific attack vectors, including insider threats or credential leaks, and some express skepticism about the capabilities of the alleged perpetrators. Comparisons are also drawn to past crypto heists and the perceived lack of consistent law enforcement action.
