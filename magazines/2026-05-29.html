🚀 Programming Languages

Coalton: Lisp Gets Statically Typed Efficiency from Haskell and OCaml

Coalton is an efficient, statically typed functional programming language that supercharges Common Lisp by taking great ideas from Haskell, Scheme, and OCaml. May 2025: Robert Smith's talk at ELS 2025.

Actionable Insight

Coalton presents an innovative bridge between the dynamic expressiveness of Lisp and the rigorous static typing and functional purity typically associated with languages like Haskell and OCaml. By compiling to and "supercharging" Common Lisp, it aims to provide Lisp developers with enhanced efficiency, compile-time guarantees, and greater reliability. This hybrid approach addresses common criticisms of traditional Lisp, potentially broadening its appeal for robust, large-scale application development.

Community Voice

The community expresses significant interest in the concept of a statically typed Lisp, with "Haskell" and "Statically Typed" being key attractors, particularly for the potential benefits in program understanding and side-effect management. However, a notable point of friction is the perceived difficulty in getting started with Coalton, especially for those unfamiliar with Common Lisp. There's also a curiosity about how Coalton implements features like Haskell's IO Monad and whether similar typed Lisp experiences could be achieved by adapting existing languages like OCaml.
