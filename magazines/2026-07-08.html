EU Parliament Votes to Revive Controversial Chat Control Surveillance Plan

Showdown in Strasbourg: The unexpected return of Chat Control 1.0 | heise online The European Parliament cleared the way for a renewed extension of the so-called “Chat Control” on Tuesday afternoon. With a narrow majority of 331 to 304 votes and eleven abstentions, the MEPs voted for an urgency motion that Parliament President Roberta Metsola had put on the agenda at short notice at the behest of the member states and the EPP group. This allows Parliament to vote again on the controversial plan on Thursday, its last session before the summer break. The aim of this maneuver: to reinstate the transitional regulation for Chat Control, which expired in April .

Actionable Insight

This narrow parliamentary vote signals a concerning push to revive the controversial "Chat Control" initiative in the EU, aiming to reinstate an expired regulation for mass surveillance of private communications. The urgent, short-notice nature of the vote highlights a determined effort to circumvent public and legislative scrutiny, raising alarm bells for digital privacy and fundamental rights across the bloc. Its potential passage could significantly impact the future of encrypted messaging and set a dangerous precedent for government oversight in the EU.

Community Voice

The Hacker News community is overwhelmingly critical and frustrated with the passage of Chat Control, viewing it as an undemocratic manipulation of legislative procedures where unpopular laws are repeatedly advanced until they pass. There's significant concern about the far-reaching negative implications of this regulation, both within and outside the EU, alongside dismay at public apathy and a perceived lack of understanding regarding its severe consequences.
