CheapSecurity Offers Lightweight, Self-Hosted CCTV for Linux SBCs

Source: original article

GitHub - gmrandazzo/CheapSecurity · GitHub

Actionable Insight

CheapSecurity is a Python-based, self-hosted CCTV solution designed for Linux Single Board Computers, focusing on lightweight operation. It integrates V4L2 for video capture, applies image processing for enhanced night vision, and uses basic frame differencing for motion detection. The system provides web streaming, video recording, and notification capabilities via Telegram or email.

Community Voice

The community frequently compares CheapSecurity to established projects like Motion and Frigate, questioning its unique value proposition. Users express concerns about finding suitable USB cameras for SBCs, highlighting common webcam limitations in enclosures, focus, and low-light performance. There's also skepticism regarding the effectiveness of its basic frame-differencing motion detection, with users reporting false positives from similar systems. Additionally, some users requested UI screenshots or GIFs, and others sought camera recommendations for Raspberry Pi setups.

Web Privacy 🍪

EU Commission Proposes Browser-Level Privacy Settings to End Cookie Banners

Source: original article

The EU Commission has finally proposed a solution: set your privacy preferences in the browser once, and never see another banner. Unfortunately, the tracking industry is pushing back – and so far, they’ve been successful. But the law is clear: online tracking is prohibited by default. Therefore, the tracking industry needs you to waive your rights. This results in up to 90% of people saying “YES” – even though only around 3% actually want to be tracked online.

Actionable Insight

The EU Commission has proposed a browser-based solution for privacy preferences, aiming to eliminate persistent cookie banners and simplify user consent. This initiative faces strong opposition from the tracking industry, which currently leverages dark patterns to secure user agreement for tracking. The current system often leads to a high rate of consent despite a low genuine desire for online tracking, highlighting a significant gap between user preference and industry practice.

Community Voice

The community questions if the proposed browser-level solution will effectively address 'legitimate interest' loopholes and advocates for simpler 'refuse all' options or default cookie isolation. Many commenters believe current cookie banners do not constitute informed consent and express frustration over the slow implementation of robust privacy controls. Some users rely on browser-native protections as a workaround, while others fundamentally argue that tracking should simply cease, eliminating the need for banners entirely.

London Gatwick Launches Robotic Airport Parking Service

Source: original article

London Gatwick introduces robotic parking service London Gatwick has become the first UK airport to introduce robotic parking, allowing passengers to leave their car in a private drop-off cabin. Autonomous robots park it in a secure storage area and car owners don’t need to hand over their keys. The new service, launched in partnership with Stanley Robotics, is now available to book ahead of its first customer journeys in August. Eliminating the hassle of searching for a parking space, the technology gets passengers from their car to the terminal more quickly while making more efficient use of airport parking capacity.

Actionable Insight

London Gatwick is the first UK airport to introduce a robotic parking service, partnering with Stanley Robotics. Passengers drop off their cars in a private cabin, and autonomous robots park them in a secure area, eliminating the need to hand over keys or search for spaces. This innovation aims to streamline the airport experience, getting passengers to the terminal faster while optimizing parking capacity.

Community Voice

Community reaction is mixed, with some users expressing skepticism about the process, such as the need to park in a garage first or concerns about car alarms being triggered. Source: original article

US prosecutors charge Atlanta man after GrapheneOS phone wipes itself during airport search | TechSpot Serving tech enthusiasts for over 25 years. TechSpot means tech analysis and advice you can trust . A hot potato: A federal case in Atlanta is raising questions about a privacy-focused mobile operating system, with prosecutors arguing that its features were used to erase evidence. The US Department of Justice is attempting to prosecute Atlanta resident Sam Tunick under a federal statute that makes it a crime to destroy property in an effort to prevent it from being seized.

Actionable Insight

A US citizen faces federal charges after his GrapheneOS phone automatically wiped during an airport search, prompting prosecutors to argue its privacy features were used to destroy evidence. This case highlights the legal complexities surrounding privacy-focused operating systems and the potential legal consequences of using features designed to protect data during government scrutiny. It also raises broader questions about the extent of government power at borders and the rights of individuals to digital privacy.

Community Voice

Community discussion centers on the legal implications of using duress PINs or self-wiping phones during border searches, with many questioning the extent of government power at national borders versus individual privacy rights. Commenters suggest technical workarounds like wiping devices before travel and restoring from backup, or using decoy operating systems. There's also concern that the charges may be politically motivated, given the individual's alleged association with the 'Cop City' protest movement.
