Demystifying Docker: Containers Are Restricted Processes, Not Virtual Machines

Source: original article

I decided to make my own container runtime for fun, and even though I’m still early on, I’ve learned that Docker is weird . Here’s some weird things I learned about Docker. Unlike VMs, containers are just processes with special restrictions on them. Your kernel is still running that process! And if you know where to look, you can even find that process in /proc.

Actionable Insight

The article reveals that containers are essentially isolated processes with special restrictions, not full virtual machines, running directly on the host kernel. This fundamental distinction clarifies Docker's lightweight nature and its reliance on existing Linux kernel features for resource isolation and process management.

Community Voice

The Hacker News community generally acknowledges Docker's significant contribution through its developer experience and the Dockerfile concept, rather than the novelty of its underlying container technology. While many praise its portability, some highlight concerns regarding security and bloat, leading to preferences for alternatives like Podman. Commenters also appreciate the demystification of complex systems and offer technical insights into Docker's implementation details.
