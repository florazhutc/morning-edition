Research Identifies Two Distinct Progenitor Cell Lineages in Human Brain Development

Source: original article

Human brain is two separate organs, Stanford Medicine-led research finds Skip to main content Skip to section navigation

Actionable Insight

Stanford-led research reveals that the human brain develops from two distinct neural ectoderm progenitor populations, one for the fore/midbrain and another for the hindbrain, which never overlap. This finding challenges prior assumptions about brain development and suggests a parallel formation of brain regions. A key outcome is a new technique for growing brain stem cells in vitro, promising advancements in neurological disease research.

Community Voice

Commenters debate the article's framing, with some criticizing the 'human brain is two separate organs' headline as clickbait and preferring the more precise description of distinct progenitor lineages. Many emphasize that the most impactful aspect of the research is the development of a new technique for growing brain stem cells in vitro, which could significantly aid future research into neurological diseases like ALS. There's also an acknowledgment that this fundamental discovery highlights gaps in basic biological knowledge. The research is noted to be a bioRxiv preprint.

AI Security 🔒

AI Model Weight Exfiltration Faces Technical and Security Barriers

Source: Hacker News / Algolia context

Community discussion highlights: the tokens are generated by hardware with secure enclaves (encrypted weights) and then sent over a network to some remote CPU where they can manifest an effect. it's not much different during training. how exactly are they supposed to exfiltrate their weights? you might as well instruct your agent to try and hack their airgapped dev infrastructure responsible for loading the weights and encryption keys.

Actionable Insight

The exfiltration of AI model weights by an agent is largely deemed impractical due to fundamental architectural and security measures. Weights are typically encrypted and locked within secure hardware enclaves, with inference machines isolated from environments where agents might attempt data exfiltration. This design makes direct access and transmission of model parameters by an AI agent exceedingly difficult, akin to breaching airgapped infrastructure.

Community Voice

The community largely agrees that an AI exfiltrating its own weights is highly improbable, citing secure enclaves, encrypted weights, and the architectural separation between inference machines and environments where agents can make tool calls. While some comments playfully explore the premise of an 'agent-ready' system, practical attempts by agents to access weights were reported to fail. Broader concerns about API abuse, storage costs, and preventing misuse of such a system were also raised, alongside skepticism regarding the real-world implications of 'uncontrollable' models in this context.
