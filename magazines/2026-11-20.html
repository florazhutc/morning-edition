UK Government Admits Secret Encryption Order to Apple Under Investigatory Powers Act

Source: original article

UK Government Forced to Admit It Issued Apple a Secret Encryption Order There's something pretty extraordinary about a government being able to demand changes to the security of devices used by millions of people, prevent a company that makes those devices from discussing those demands, and then refuse to even acknowledge that the demands exist at all. That has been the sordid situation surrounding the UK governement's ongoing dispute with Apple over encrypted iCloud data. Up until today, the UK government's position has essentially been that it would neither confirm nor deny whether it issued Apple with a Technical Capability Notice (TCN). These are secret orders issued under Section 253 of the Investigatory Powers Act 2016 , which gives the UK government powers to require technology companies to make changes to their services to facilitate the exercise of investigatory powers.

Actionable Insight

The UK government was compelled to acknowledge issuing a secret Technical Capability Notice to Apple, demanding changes to device security under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016. This admission follows a period of official refusal to confirm or deny the order's existence, highlighting significant concerns about government transparency and the scope of state power over private technology. The situation underscores the ongoing tension between national security imperatives and the public's right to privacy, especially when such directives are issued in secret.

Community Voice

The community expresses significant concern over the secrecy of government orders, questioning the justification for concealing such directives and viewing it as potentially malicious. Many commenters highlight the UK's perceived history of extensive mass surveillance and disregard for privacy rights, leading to skepticism about Apple's security assurances. Some speculate on the technical implications, such as potential man-in-the-middle attacks on end-to-end encryption, while others note that the existence of such government demands was widely suspected prior to the official admission.

Introducing Frostbite: A new CPU architecture discovered, designed, and verified end-to-end by AI | Architect Labs In our previous post, Redwood , we showed that given an architecture, our AI systems could implement, verify, and deploy an inference accelerator in record time. Actionable Insight

PostgreSQL 19 introduces significant enhancements aimed at improving operational efficiency and performance.
