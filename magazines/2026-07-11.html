Although a majority of voting Members of the European Parliament (MEPs) actually opposed the regulation ( 314 against, 276 in favor, 17 abstentions ), the motion to reject it failed to secure the required absolute majority of 361 votes. As a result, mass scanning is now permitted again until 2028.

Actionable Insight

The EU Parliament's decision to greenlight "Chat Control 1.0" marks a significant blow to digital privacy and civil liberties in Europe, authorizing suspicionless mass scanning of private communications despite a majority of MEPs actually opposing it. This procedural victory for surveillance advocates underscores how parliamentary mechanisms can override democratic will, setting a worrying precedent for fundamental rights under the guise of child protection until 2028.

Community Voice

The Hacker News community overwhelmingly condemns the EU Parliament's greenlighting of Chat Control 1.0, viewing it as a significant setback for digital privacy and a deeply undemocratic process. Commenters express outrage that the legislation was passed through controversial parliamentary tactics despite having been rejected previously and despite a majority of voting MEPs opposing it in the final vote.
