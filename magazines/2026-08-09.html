Software Development 💻

The Claim 'Coding Was Never the Hard Part' Insults Programmers, Article Argues

Source: original article

"Code was never the hard part" is an insult to all programmers — Senko Rašić “Code was never the hard part” is an insult to all programmers The software development profession is in the midst of upheaval. Nobody knows how the AI revolution will play out in the end, but it is clear many aspects of work and life will be transformed—including programming. One of the comments I hear often lately boils down to “ LLMs may be good at coding, but software was never the hard part ” and “ coding is easy, it's figuring out what to code that's hard ”.

Actionable Insight

The article contends that the assertion 'coding was never the hard part' undervalues the intricate skills and effort involved in programming, especially amidst the rise of AI coding tools. It challenges the notion that only 'figuring out what to code' is difficult, suggesting this perspective is an insult to the craft of software development. This viewpoint highlights a potential misrepresentation of the programmer's role and the complexity of their work.

Community Voice

The community offers varied opinions, with some agreeing that in highly complex domains, coding can be less challenging than the underlying problem-solving. However, many argue that the historical demand and high salaries for programmers reflect the inherent difficulty and the 'invisible hats' developers wear beyond mere syntax. Some commenters suggest the 'coding is easy' observation is often misinterpreted or represents a 'post-LLM coding romanticization.' A common distinction is made between 'coding' (encoding ideas) and 'programming' (the deeper understanding, architectural challenges, and validation of ideas), with observations that even with AI-generated code, significant skill differences in delivering solutions persist among developers.

Actionable Insight

Denmark is reintroducing oral defenses for written assignments in high schools, a move primarily aimed at combating AI-driven plagiarism. This approach reverts to historical educational practices, emphasizing direct student engagement and understanding over solely written submissions. While potentially increasing authenticity, it also introduces challenges related to scalability and grading subjectivity compared to written assessments.

Community Voice

The community highlights that oral defenses are a traditional method, already common in Danish higher education and historically. While effective for ensuring student understanding and combating AI plagiarism, concerns are raised regarding the potential loss of efficiency compared to written assessments, challenges with scalability for large classes, and increased subjectivity in grading. Some educators are exploring alternative methods like 'AI Authenticity Audits' for project-based work.

Actionable Insight

Source: original article

Actionable Insight

The closure of Gentoo's Bugzilla due to AI bot scraper overload exemplifies a growing challenge for public web services. As AI models voraciously consume data for training, open-source projects and other public resources face increased strain, often leading to service disruptions or the implementation of restrictive access measures. This trend underscores the need for sustainable solutions to balance data accessibility with server integrity in the AI era.

Community Voice

The community discusses the prevalence of AI scrapers, with some questioning the identity of the bots beyond major AI labs and noting similar issues faced by other public content providers. Proposed solutions range from micropayments or in-browser crypto mining to more technical approaches like directing bot traffic to specific servers or implementing basic authentication. Some commenters express skepticism, viewing the issue as a form of "DDoS'ing for extortion" aimed at creating a more restricted internet, while others wonder about the specific value of scraping Gentoo's bug reports.
